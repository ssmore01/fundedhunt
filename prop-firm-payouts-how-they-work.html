Prop Firm Payouts: How They Actually Work and When You Get Paid

📅 April 19, 2026⏱ 14 min read✍️ FundedHunt

You passed your challenge. You are live on a funded account. You are making money. Now comes the question every new funded trader asks and almost nobody gives a straight answer to: when do you actually get paid, and how does it work?

Prop firm payout systems are more complex than most firms make them sound in their marketing. There are profit split calculations, minimum payout thresholds, processing times, payment method restrictions, and a handful of conditions that can delay or outright block your withdrawal. Understanding all of this before you trade a single lot on a funded account is not optional. It is essential.

This guide covers everything. No marketing language. Just the facts you need.

What a Profit Split Actually Means

Every prop firm advertises a profit split. You will see numbers like 80%, 85%, 90%, or even 100%. Here is what that actually means in practice.

If your funded account has an 80% profit split and you generate $5,000 in profit during your payout cycle, you receive $4,000. The firm keeps $1,000. That is the basic calculation.

Where it gets more complicated is how "profit" is defined. Most firms calculate profit based on your net gain above the initial account balance. But some firms factor in their fee structure differently, and others have specific rules around how profit is calculated when you have multiple accounts or have previously received payouts.

"The profit split percentage is the headline. What matters is how the firm defines the profit it is splitting with you."

Firms that offer 100% profit splits typically charge higher evaluation fees or have a monthly fee structure. A 100% split with a $500 evaluation fee is not necessarily better value than an 80% split with a $150 fee. Do the math based on your realistic monthly return, not the headline number.

How Payout Cycles Work

Prop firms structure payouts in cycles. There are three main models in use today.

Monthly payouts. The traditional model. You trade for a calendar month, the firm reviews at the end, and if you meet the conditions, your payout is processed. Most established firms like FTMO use this model.

Bi-weekly payouts. Some firms have moved to a 14-day cycle. You become eligible for a payout every two weeks after your first cycle completes.

On-demand or weekly payouts. A growing number of firms offer on-demand payouts after a minimum number of trading days, or weekly payouts from day one. This model is attractive but pay close attention to minimum profit thresholds before you can request.

Key Point

Most firms have a waiting period before your first payout. FTMO requires a minimum of 14 calendar days after you go live before your first withdrawal. Others require a set number of profitable trading days. This first payout waiting period is often buried in the terms and catches traders off guard.

Minimum Payout Thresholds

Almost every prop firm has a minimum amount you need to have earned before you can request a payout. This is typically between $100 and $500 depending on the firm and account size. If your profit for the cycle is below this threshold, you cannot withdraw and the balance rolls into the next cycle.

Payment Methods and Processing Times

This is where Indian traders in particular need to pay close attention. The payment method a firm supports directly affects how quickly you receive your money and what it costs you.

Wise is the most commonly recommended option for Indian traders. It supports INR, has low fees, and transfers reliably. Most Indian funded traders use Wise as their primary payout method across multiple prop firms.

Crypto payouts are fast and borderless but require you to convert to INR through an Indian exchange afterward. Keep in mind that crypto-to-INR conversions are subject to 30% tax on gains under current VDA taxation rules.

Bank wire is universally available but the slowest option at 3 to 7 days and carries the highest fees at $15 to $40 per transfer.

What Can Delay Your Payout

KYC verification not complete. Know Your Customer verification is mandatory before any firm will process a withdrawal. Complete KYC the moment you go live on a funded account, not when you first want to withdraw.

Payout requested outside the eligible window. Each firm has specific dates or conditions for when you can submit a payout request. If you request outside the window, your request queues for the next eligible date.

Account under review. Firms periodically review funded accounts, especially around payout requests. If your trading patterns flagged anything during the cycle, the review takes longer.

Minimum trading days not met. Some firms require a minimum number of trading days within a payout cycle. Read your firm's minimum trading day requirement carefully.

What Can Deny Your Payout Entirely

Consistency rule violation. If your best trading day represents more than the allowed percentage of your total profit, many firms will deny the payout and ask you to continue trading until the distribution normalises.

Rule violations discovered on review. Trading during restricted news windows, using prohibited strategies, or holding positions over the weekend when not allowed can all result in denial.

Payment method issues. Incorrect bank details, a mismatched name between your account and your payment method, or using an unsupported method in your country can all cause rejection.

The Hard Truth

Most payout denials are avoidable. They happen because traders did not read the full terms before trading. The rules around payouts are almost always in the firm's terms and conditions. There is no excuse for discovering them only at withdrawal time.

Profit Split Scaling

Several firms offer profit split scaling as part of their programme. Your split percentage increases as you demonstrate consistent performance over time. A firm might start you at 75% profit split and increase to 80% after three consecutive profitable months, then 85% after six months. If you plan to trade prop firms as a serious income stream, choose a firm with a clear scaling plan.

Fee Refunds Explained

Many prop firms advertise that the evaluation fee is refunded with your first payout. The fee refund is typically added to your first payout as a bonus amount. So if your first payout is $1,200 and your evaluation fee was $150, you receive $1,350 total. The condition is almost always that you must reach the minimum payout threshold from trading profits alone first.

Taxes on Prop Firm Payouts in India

For Indian traders, payout income from foreign prop firms falls under income from other sources or business income depending on how regularly you trade. It is taxable at your applicable income tax slab rate. The firm will not withhold tax on your behalf. The responsibility to declare it and pay tax rests entirely with you. If you are receiving regular payout income, speak to a chartered accountant who understands foreign income and FEMA compliance.

What to Compare Before Choosing a Firm

Before committing to any evaluation, get clear answers to these questions from the firm's terms or support.

When is the first payout available after going live? What is the payout cycle? What is the minimum payout threshold? What payment methods are available for Indian traders? Is there a consistency rule? Is the evaluation fee refunded and under what conditions exactly? Does the profit split increase over time?

"A 90% profit split with a painful payout process is worth less than an 80% split from a firm that pays reliably within 24 hours."

Know What You Are Getting Into Before You Trade

The payout system is the entire point of trading a prop firm account. Understanding it fully before you start is the minimum you owe yourself before putting real time and money into an evaluation.
